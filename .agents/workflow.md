# Workflow and boundaries

Read this before branching, committing or opening a PR. The authoritative
human version is [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md); shared task rules are
in [general.md](general.md).

## Git

- Always branch off **`development`**, never `main`. `main` is the stable
  release branch; `development` is where changes integrate before a version.
- Name the branch `<type>/<short-english-description>` in kebab-case, using the
  most specific type: `feat/`, `fix/`, `security/`, `chore/`, `docs/`,
  `refactor/`, `perf/`, `test/`, `ci/`, `build/`.
  Example: `fix/login-crash-empty-password`.
- Open the PR **against `development`**. Merging into `main` is reserved for
  releases.
- One logical change per PR. Do not bundle unrelated refactors, reformatting of
  untouched files, or dependency bumps — Dependabot owns those, and
  `.github/CODEOWNERS` already assigns the maintainer as reviewer.
- Resolve conflicts with `development` yourself before asking for review.
- Never rewrite published history and never force-push a shared branch.
- Commit messages, branch names and PR text in English, describing the change
  and why it was needed.

## Acceptance

A PR must meet every requirement in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) —
formatting, build, tests, no vulnerable dependencies, the Git workflow, and no
merge conflicts. Meeting them is necessary but **not sufficient**: the
maintainer has the final say and may still request changes for quality,
consistency with the architecture, or the project's direction.

## Boundaries

The **Use of AI** policy in
[CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#-use-of-ai) governs everything here:

- AI is allowed as an **assistant**. Contributions **generated by autonomous
  agents are rejected automatically.**
- The developer opening the PR is responsible for the code: its quality, its
  adherence to this architecture, and its security.

What that means for you, concretely:

- Stay inside the scope of what was asked. Do not hand over refactors,
  renames, dependency bumps or "while I was in there" fixes nobody requested —
  raise them instead and let the developer decide.
- Do not commit, push, open a PR or merge anything unless you were explicitly
  asked to. Leave the work in the working tree for review.
- Say plainly what you changed and why, and flag anything you were unsure about
  or could not verify, rather than presenting a guess as a finished result.
- Never weaken a rule to make your change pass: no new analyzer suppressions, no
  disabled tests, no widened visibility, no version bumps. If a rule genuinely
  blocks the task, stop and say so.
- The review and the merge decision belong to a human.
